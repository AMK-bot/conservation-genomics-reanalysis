The project in 90 seconds

A stable headline can sit above fragile estimates.

The headline and the magnitude are evaluated separately. Teal marks evidence that held; amber marks sensitivity that must stay visible.

Chapter 01 · Green peafowl

Pavo muticus

Stable aggregate headline · fragile population verdicts

Across three filter presets, no sampled population confidently clears Ne > 500. That does not prove every true value is below 500: wide intervals and non-convergence expose the limits of samples of 5–10 birds.

Chapter 02 · Scandinavian wolf system

Canis lupus

Stable direction · sensitive magnitude

At GERP > 4, deposited raw counts reproduce exactly across all 211 wolves and load values agree to floating-point precision. GERP thresholds move the values, while the realized-versus-masked direction holds in all eight groups.

Stable
8 of 8 groups retain their direction across all three GERP thresholds
Moves

Reproducibility

After one-time setup, one workflow command rebuilds the derived evidence chain.

The static portfolio needs no software to read. Reproducing the analysis uses a pinned environment, source-built tools, declared thresholds, and a Snakemake DAG designed for an 8 GB laptop. The recorded 162-job clean clone covers the core scientific and admissions artifacts; the newer portal has its own 10-check gate and is included in the current all target.

Scientific maturity

The strongest claim is the one the evidence can carry.

The project makes its boundary explicit: a reproducible public-data reanalysis can demonstrate analytical sensitivity and conservation risk, but not measured inbreeding depression or new field evidence.

Claim

No sampled peafowl population confidently clears Ne > 500.

Not: “all true population sizes are known to be below 500.”

Claim

Wolf genomic-risk direction survives the tested GERP thresholds.

Not: “genetic load has directly caused reduced survival or fertility.”

Context

GONE2 suggests a recent decline on a different timescale.

It uses an approximate recombination rate and does not decide the contemporary verdict.

Next evidence

For resolving the present peafowl Ne uncertainty, more independent individuals are the priority over simply adding SNPs.

Better sampling, functional evidence, and finer occurrence records would change the conclusions most.

“An uncertain estimate is not an empty result. It tells us what the present data cannot yet support.”
